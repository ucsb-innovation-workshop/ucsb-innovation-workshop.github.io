## Safety Concerns

Eye injuries can result from flying chips or broken bits. Sharp tools, chips, and burrs can cause cuts or metal splinters. Cutting tools and parts may cause burns. Moving parts can pinch or entangle clothing, hair, or jewelry. Poor housekeeping creates tripping and cutting hazards for other users. **Do not leave the machine running unattended.**

## Overview

### Safe Materials to Drill

- **Aluminum:** Use lubricant (WD-40).
- **Brass:** Use lubricant.
- **Plastic:** Do not use lubricant.
- **Wood:** Do not use lubricant.

### Sequence of Operation

#### Before Drilling

1. Check that you are complying with all PPE guidelines.
2. Lay out and center-punch the holes you wish to drill.
3. Collect the tools needed for the operation. Inspect them to ensure they are sharp and in good condition. **Do not use end mills or Dremel bits in the drill press.**
4. Insert the bit into the drill chuck, tighten it with the chuck key, and remove the chuck key before continuing. **Do not tighten the chuck by powering on the machine or leave the chuck key in the chuck.** Both are extremely dangerous practices.
5. Align the holes with the drill bit and secure the part using the vise or drill bed clamp. Do not use custom fixtures or setups without permission from lab staff. **Do not hold the material with your hands.**
6. Adjust the worktable to the appropriate height and lock it securely.
7. Check that the tool can spin freely and is clear of the part.
8. Consult the chart to determine the proper feed and speed for the hole and material. Adjust the machine to the appropriate speed.
9. Remove extra tools and parts from the drill press. Check again that everything is clear of rotating and moving parts.
10. Apply cutting lubricant to the part where appropriate for the material.

#### Drilling

1. Turn on the machine. **Do not leave it running unattended.**
2. Use peck drilling to remove material in 0.05-inch increments.
3. Remove chips and swarf with a brush. **Do not use a rag or your bare hands to remove chips.**
4. Apply additional cutting fluid as necessary throughout the operation.
5. Reduce pressure when breaking through to prevent damage to the tool and part. Use a backing board to prevent the part from blowing out.
6. Turn off the machine when finished. **Do not attempt to slow it by hand.**

#### After Drilling

1. Carefully remove the part and tool from the machine. They may be hot; check before grabbing them.
2. Use a brush and/or rag to clean chips, shavings, and oil from the drill press and surrounding area.
3. Return all tools to their proper locations, power down the drill press, and tidy the work area.

## Good Drilling Practices

- Work from a drawing and project plan.
- Lay out the workpiece properly before beginning.
- Use additional light to illuminate the workpiece.
- Step up to larger drill sizes. The diameter of the smaller drill bit should be no thicker than the web (distance between flutes) of the larger drill bit.
- Support the tool with one hand when removing it from the chuck so it does not fall.
