# Vapor Silanization Rig Safe Operating Procedures

## Location

The vapor silanization rig is located in the first fume hood on your left when entering the microfluidics lab in Elings 3430.

---

## Safety

- Trimethylchlorosilane is flammable, toxic, and reacts with water vapor. It can make you sick if inhaled and ignite if it reacts with air or water vapor.
- Use trimethylchlorosilane only in the vapor silanization rig inside a fume hood.
- Do not let the pressure inside the chamber exceed 15 in Hg.
- The silane valve must always be opened in a nitrogen atmosphere.
- Wear protective eyewear, a lab coat, and gloves.
- Keep the fume hood closed when you are not working inside it.
- Consult the Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS) before handling the silane.

The new glass reservoir has approximately 14 times the free liquid surface area of the previous reservoir. **TMCS evaporates roughly 14 times faster than before!** It will reach atmospheric pressure and leak within 2 hours if the silane valve is left open.

**Open the silane valve only for the first 5 minutes of deposition.** Then fully close the valve and continue the deposition for the required duration.

---

## Trimethylchlorosilane

**Synonyms:**

- TMCS
- Trimethylchlorosilane
- Trimethylsilyl chloride

**Formula:** C₃H₉ClSi  
**Molecular Weight:** 108.64 g/mol  
**CAS Number:** 75-77-4  
**EC Number:** 200-900-5  

This protocol is intended for gas-phase monolayer deposition of a silane to serve as a counter-adhesion agent for glass or silicon substrates. Trimethylchlorosilane is referred to as “silane” throughout this protocol. It can be deposited onto silicon and glass substrates and can etch metal oxides, such as alumina.

---

## Operating Guidelines

1. Add yourself to the Silane Vapor Deposition Rig Log.

   <img src="/assets/d.jpg" alt="Silane Vapor Deposition Rig Log" width="35%">

2. Place your wafer inside the chamber. Check the O-ring seal. If dirty, wipe it with a Kimwipe. Ensure that the O-ring is fully seated in the groove.

3. Pull a vacuum by turning on the pump and then opening the red “Vacuum” valve. When the pressure reads approximately 15 in Hg, turn the pump off and close the valve. Test the vacuum by waiting a minute or two and verifying that the pressure reading remains steady.

4. Turn on the N₂ supply on the side of the fume hood. Turn the knob only once or twice.

5. Open the silane chamber N₂ valve until the pressure reads approximately 5 in Hg. Then turn on the pump and open the vacuum valve until the pressure reads 15 in Hg. Repeat this cycle 5 times. Turn off the pump when finished.

6. Turn the silane flask valve counterclockwise one full turn; the green masking tape should be on top. The pressure should begin to slowly drop. Wait approximately 5 minutes, then close the silane flask valve. There is now plenty of vapor in the chamber.

   <img src="/assets/k.png" alt="Silane flask valve" width="35%">

7. Flip the sign to **IN USE**.

8. Close the fume hood and set a timer for 40 minutes.

9. When your cycle is complete, update the logbook with your end time.

10. Open the N₂ valve until the pressure reads 5 in Hg. Open the vacuum valve until the pressure reads 15 in Hg. Repeat 5 times to purge the chamber again.

11. Close the N₂ valve on the side of the fume hood.

12. Remove your wafer.

13. Flip the sign back to **AVAILABLE** and close the hood.
