    Bandsaw Safe Operating Procedure

    Safety Concerns

    Serious injury and potential dismemberment can occur from contact with the moving saw blade.

    Eye injuries can result from flying chips or broken bits. Cuts can occur from contacting sharp tools or exposed chips and burrs. Burns can result from hot cutting tools or parts. Metal splinters can result from exposed burrs or chips. Serious injury can result from pinching or entanglement in moving parts.

    Poor housekeeping creates tripping and cutting hazards for other users. Do not leave the machine running unattended.

    Eye protection, closed-toe shoes, long pants, and protective clothing must be worn when using this machine to prevent injury. Long hair must be tied back and jewelry must be removed before operating this machine to prevent entanglement.

    Training

    Training Requirements

    Before using this machine, users must:

    • Take and pass the Machine Shop Safety test found on the ME Labs GauchoSpace page.
    • Read the Drill Press document on the Innovation Workshop GauchoSpace page.
    • Attend Innovation Workshop general machine-tool training.
    • Complete Innovation Workshop bandsaw-specific training.
    • Review additional documents found on the GauchoSpace page.

    Safety

    Physical Safety

    • The saw blade can cause serious injury and dismemberment.
    • Keep fingers at least 3 inches away from the saw blade at all times.
    • Keep the saw blade cover no more than 1/4 inch above the surface of the stock to minimize blade exposure.
    • Exercise caution when cutting large stock. Ensure there is ample clearance around the machine.
    • Keep the work area clean. A cluttered work area presents multiple hazards.
    • The machine is kept locked until the online reservation system can be implemented. Lab staff will keep it unlocked during the workday and can unlock it upon request if needed.
    • Use the exhaust system and shop vacuum to prevent inhalation of particulates.

    Safe Operating Procedure

    Sequence of Operation

    1. Move the saw out from the alcove if cutting large stock.
    2. Adjust the rip fence to the proper length for the cut. Lock the rip fence in place.
    3. Adjust the saw blade cover to 1/4 inch above the workpiece. Tighten the blade cover in place.
    4. Power on the motor controller. The power switch is located on the side of the unit.
    5. Select the proper material setting on the motor controller. Press once to select the material group, then press the button to cycle through to the appropriate material setting.
    6. Press On to start the bandsaw.
    7. Turn the exhaust system on.
    8. Hold the material securely while keeping fingers at least 3 inches away from the saw blade. Use the push stick if necessary.
    9. Push the material through the saw blade evenly and slowly. If the saw blade chatters or screams, ease off the feed pressure.
    10. Once the cut is complete, press Off on the motor controller to stop the bandsaw. Do not reach for the parts at this time. Turn the exhaust system off as well.
    11. Once the saw blade has come to a complete stop, remove your parts. Use a brush or shop vacuum to clean up any remaining dust and chips.
    12. Extend the saw blade cover to cover the entire blade.
    13. Power off the motor controller. The motor controller stays illuminated for approximately 15 seconds after switching the power off.
    14. Return the bandsaw to the alcove.
    15. Clean up any remaining dust and debris from the floor.

    Detailed Procedure

    Supplies

    • Bandsaw blades are kept in the cabinet of the bandsaw stand. Notify lab staff by emailing ‪bdincau@ucsb.edu if you break a bandsaw blade. Lab staff will replace it.
    • The resaw bar assembly is kept in the cabinet of the bandsaw stand. Use the resaw bar assembly to prevent the bandsaw blade from walking or moving during the cut.

    Recommended Blade Selection and Speed

    In general, at least three cutting teeth should be in contact with the material being cut at all times. Thinner materials therefore require a finer-tooth blade than thicker materials.

    Please see lab staff for instruction on changing bandsaw blades.

    The bandsaw motor connects to the blade drive through a two-speed belt drive. The belt must be in the low range when cutting stainless steel and titanium. All other materials may be cut with the belt in the high range.

    The controller has four pre-programmed material categories:

    • Wood
    • Metal
    • Non-ferrous metals
    • Plastic

    Within each category, four material types are programmed. Preset speeds are listed in two units: surface feet per minute (SFPM) and revolutions per minute (RPM).

    The conversion factor between SFPM and RPM changes depending on the position of the belt on the drive pulley.

    The speed-adjustment dial on the controller can be used to increase or decrease the speed from the preset levels. If the saw is making excessive noise, try lowering the speed.

    Bandsaw Feeds and Speeds

    General rule of thumb for TPI: Always have a minimum of 3 teeth engaged in the material at any given time.

    Material Blade TPI SFPM
    Aluminum 24 TPI @ 1 mm, <10 TPI @ >6 mm 1000 FPM
    Steel (low carbon) 20 TPI @ 3 mm, <14 TPI @ >6.5 mm <250 FPM
    Steel (stainless/Chromoly/high carbon) 28 TPI @ 1 mm, <14 TPI @ >6 mm <125 FPM
    Plastic 24 TPI @ 1 mm, <10 TPI @ >6 mm 2500 FPM
    Wood 24 TPI @ 1 mm, <10 TPI @ >6 mm 4000 FPM
